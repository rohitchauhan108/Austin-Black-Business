Remembering Tommie Lee (T. L.) Wyatt: A Mentor, a Messenger, and a Legend of Black Austin

T. L. Wyatt leaves a lasting legacy as a publisher, civic leader, mentor, and steadfast voice for Austin’s Black community.

Black Business Journal Editorial Team

History & Community News Desk • September 18, 2026

Executive Summary & Key Takeaways

  • •T. L. Wyatt passed away peacefully on January 9, 2026, at age 88.
  • •He co-founded The Villager newspaper in 1973 to tell the positive, truthful stories of Black Austin.
  • •Wyatt served as a civic leader on boards and commissions across Austin and Texas.
  • •His legacy continues through the journalists, institutions, and young people he mentored.

Tommie Lee (T. L.) Wyatt, a steadfast voice for Austin’s Black community and a lifelong champion of truthful, empowering journalism, passed away peacefully in his sleep on January 9, 2026, at the AVIR at Park Bend facility in Austin, Texas. He was 88 years old and was in the presence of his son, Thomas L. Wyatt.

Born on July 27, 1937, in Point Blank, Texas, Wyatt earned a football scholarship and graduated from Bishop College in Marshall, Texas, with a bachelor’s degree in Business Administration. His early life was shaped by discipline, determination, and a belief in possibility.

In 1962, Wyatt moved to Austin, entering a city still constrained by segregation. He remembered East Austin for its brilliance: a vibrant, self-sustaining Black community filled with thriving businesses, cultural pride, and deep-rooted connections.

In 1973, Wyatt, his wife Barbara, and a close friend co-founded The Villager newspaper with a bold mission: to tell the positive, truthful stories of Black Austin. When his co-founders moved on, Wyatt carried the paper forward and insisted that Black communities deserved coverage reflecting their full humanity.

Wyatt often said, “The community needed a voice.” Through The Villager, his magazine Loquacity, and KAZI, Austin’s only Black radio station at the time, he answered that call. He understood the importance of Black newspapers as places of accessibility, memory, and belonging.

Beyond journalism, Wyatt served on the Private Industry Council, the Austin Cable Commission, and the East 11th Street Village Association. He was a founding member of the Texas Publishers Association and a steadfast member of the National Newspaper Publishers Association. Through the Youth Brigade and relationships with major corporations, he helped bring education, opportunity, and financial resources into the community.

To those who knew him, T. L. Wyatt was more than a publisher or civic leader. He was a mentor, connector, and joyful presence who built relationships through warmth, curiosity, and optimism. His vision, persistence, and love for East Austin created a legacy that will continue to guide generations.

Tommie Lee Wyatt is survived by his son, granddaughter, and great-grandchildren. Visitation will be held at Cook-Walden/Capital Parks Funeral Home on January 20, 2026, from 4:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m. Funeral services will take place at the same location on January 21, 2026, from 10:00 a.m. to 11:00 a.m., followed by committal services at Cook-Walden Capital Parks Cemetery.
